Series Viewer Guide

Use the right report, for the right cricket decision.

Once your series access is approved, the analytics flow is straightforward: open the series, search the player, and choose the report that matches your decision. Player Assessment is for selection and development. Player Intelligence is for opposition planning and match strategy.

Start here: Sign in with the email that was approved for the series.
Then: Open Analytics, choose the series, and search the player.
1. Open the workspace Analytics becomes useful only after the right series is selected.

Make sure you are inside the approved series before you read any player output.

2. Choose the report Assessment asks “How good is this player for us?”

Use it for selection, development, role fit, and coach or selector reviews.

3. Change the lens Intelligence asks “How do we play against this player?”

Use it for threat planning, matchup decisions, pressure reads, and tactical execution.

4. Take action The report is not the finish line. It should drive a cricket decision.

Turn the read into a shortlist, coaching note, bowling plan, batting plan, or match-up call.

Step By Step

Your First Run In Analytics

A new approved viewer should be able to do this in under ten minutes without any technical setup.

01

Sign in with the approved email

Use the same email address that was granted series access. If the approval was saved in advance, that same email should unlock the series once you sign in.

02

Open Analytics and pick the correct series

The same player name can exist in different competitions or age groups. Always confirm the series first so the report reflects the correct competition context.

03

Search the player you want to review

Search by name, then open the player route. If more than one match appears, choose the player in the series you actually want to assess.

04

Open Player Assessment for selection or development

This is the report for coaches, selectors, and development leads who want to understand overall player value, role fit, and current-readiness signal.

05

Open Player Intelligence for tactical planning

This is the report for opposition preparation. Read it when the question is how to bowl to the player, how to use a bowler against them, or where the threat appears in a match.

06

Turn the output into a cricket action

Finish with a decision: shortlist for selection, development watch, batting plan, bowling plan, matchup call, phase strategy, or role allocation.

Report Types

When To Use Each Report

The easiest way to avoid confusion is to start with the decision you are making, then choose the report.

Player Assessment

Use this when the player is being judged for your side.

This is the selector and development report. It helps answer whether the player should be selected, watched, backed, or worked on further.

  • Best for coaches, selectors, academies, and development reviews.
  • Focuses on role fit, current series performance, overall public profile, and readiness signal.
  • Useful for comparing players, understanding all-round value, and identifying development gaps.
  • Read the summary, series tables, overall tables, and confidence-style signal together.
Player Intelligence

Use this when the player is an opposition problem to solve.

This is the tactical report. It helps answer how to play against the player, where the main threat sits, and what live watchouts matter most.

  • Best for captains, analysts, coaches, and opposition prep meetings.
  • Focuses on threat type, weakness type, matchup reads, phase threat, and dismissal patterns.
  • Useful before a game, during planning reviews, and while shaping bowling or batting tactics.
  • Read it as a game-plan note, not as a selection-grade evaluation.
Simple Rule

Decision Matrix

If you are unsure which report to open, use the rule below. It keeps the workflow simple for first-time viewers.

Open this report based on the question you are asking.

Question Open Why
Should we select or keep tracking this player? Player Assessment It summarizes current value, role fit, and development-readiness for your setup.
What is the player’s strongest current contribution? Player Assessment It is designed to judge what the player gives your team right now across disciplines.
How should we bowl to this batter or use a bowler against them? Player Intelligence It is built for matchup and tactical planning against the player.
Where is the player most dangerous in a match? Player Intelligence It highlights threat shape, phase tendency, and pressure signals.
If the player is ours, start with Assessment. Think selection, role clarity, development planning, or coach review.
If the player is theirs, start with Intelligence. Think opposition planning, tactical usage, matchups, and live watchouts.
Do not mix the two jobs. Assessment is not an opposition plan. Intelligence is not a full selection report.
What Good Looks Like

Your First Useful Output

A strong first session is not “I opened the page.” It is “I ended with a decision.” These are the right next moves once the reports are open.

For Player Assessment

Finish with a selection or development note. Example: strong consideration, keep tracking, role watch, bowling all-rounder with batting support, or batting prospect needing stronger evidence.

Best final output: a selector or coach summary you can actually act on.

For Player Intelligence

Finish with a tactical instruction. Example: attack with right-arm pace early, hold a spinner for the middle phase, squeeze with dot-ball pressure, or protect a bowler from this matchup.

Best final output: a bowling plan, batting plan, or matchup instruction.

What not to do

Do not read a single line in isolation. The summaries are useful, but the tables, phase context, threat notes, and supporting sections should confirm the same story before you make a decision.

Use the report as evidence, not just as a headline.

If something looks incomplete

Some players have lighter public data or incomplete historical coverage. In that case, rely more on the current series context and treat the report as directional rather than final.

Low sample does not mean unusable. It means read with care.
